The 369 method uses three short writing moments each day. You write one intention three times in the morning, six in the afternoon, and nine in the evening.

Some people call it a manifestation method. A grounded interpretation is simpler: repetition gives one idea regular attention. It creates space to notice choices, obstacles, and progress.

## Start with one useful intention

Choose something that can guide your behaviour. Keep it specific, believable, and connected to an action you control.

“I give my important work one focused hour today” is easier to use than a guaranteed outcome. The sentence points toward a choice you can make.

## Morning: write it three times

Use the first repetition to remember the intention. Use the second to picture the next practical step. Use the third to decide when you will act.

This can take less than two minutes. The point is clarity, not perfect handwriting or a special mood.

## Afternoon: return six times

Pause and notice what has happened since morning. Write the intention six times, then add one short observation.

Ask what helped, what distracted you, and what the next small action could be. Change the wording if it no longer fits the day.

## Evening: close with nine times

Use the final round to reflect, not to judge. Write the intention nine times, then name one thing you learned.

Progress may be an action, a clearer decision, or honest evidence that the intention needs to change.

## Make the ritual sustainable

Keep the session short. Attach it to moments that already exist, such as breakfast, an afternoon break, and getting ready for bed. Missing a round does not ruin anything. Continue at the next useful moment.

Stellar guides the three phases, keeps the writing together, and can remind you to return. It does not guarantee that repetition will create an external outcome. The value comes from attention, reflection, and consistent action.
