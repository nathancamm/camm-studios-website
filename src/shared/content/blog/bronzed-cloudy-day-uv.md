Yes. You can get sunburnt and accumulate UV damage on a cloudy or cool day. Cloud cover can reduce ultraviolet radiation, but it does not reliably block it. The World Health Organization says UV levels can remain high with cloud cover, while the Bureau of Meteorology warns that UV is not related to temperature.

The practical rule is simple: check the local UV Index rather than judging conditions by brightness, cloud, or heat.

## Why cloud does not make a day UV safe

Cloud changes how much UV reaches the ground, but the effect varies with the conditions. A grey sky is therefore not a consistent layer of protection.

UV also cannot be seen or felt. A cool breeze, mild temperature, or lack of strong sunlight can make exposure easy to underestimate. The UV Index is designed to describe the radiation level directly.

## Check the UV Index and protection times

The UV Index runs from low values upward. A higher number means greater potential for skin and eye damage and less time for harm to occur.

In Australia, official guidance recommends sun protection when the UV Index reaches 3 or above. The Bureau of Meteorology forecast includes the expected UV level and local sun protection times. Check those before heading outside and keep in mind that conditions can change.

## Use several forms of protection

When protection is recommended, Cancer Council advises combining five measures:

- Cover skin with protective clothing.
- Apply broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen.
- Wear a broad-brimmed hat.
- Seek shade.
- Wear sunglasses that protect the eyes.

Sunscreen is one part of the plan, not a reason to stay in direct sun for longer. Follow the product label and official guidance for application and reapplication.

## A tan is still UV damage

A suntan is a visible response to UV damage, not evidence that exposure was safe. You do not need to burn for damage to occur, and a so-called base tan does not make intentional UV exposure safe.

That remains true on a cloudy day. If UV reaches unprotected skin, tanning and other damage can still occur.
