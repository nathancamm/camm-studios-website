Write a personal affirmation as a short, honest sentence about a direction you value and an action you can influence. It should be easy to remember and useful at a real decision point. It does not need to predict the future or pretend that a difficult situation has already disappeared.

A practical starting formula is: **I am practising [quality] by [specific action] today.**

## Start with the job the sentence needs to do

Choose one moment when you want a clearer cue. It might be starting focused work, speaking with more care, keeping a boundary, or returning to a routine.

“I am successful” is broad and hard to act on. “I am practising focus by giving the first 20 minutes to one task” points to a choice you can make.

## Keep the language believable

An affirmation does not have to begin with a sweeping “I am” claim. If that wording feels false or forced, soften it without losing the direction:

- “I am learning to ask for the time I need.”
- “Today I can take one useful step.”
- “I want to respond with patience, and I can pause before I answer.”
- “I am practising consistency, not perfection.”

The aim is not maximum positivity. It is a sentence you can read without having to argue with it.

## Make one part controllable

Avoid making another person, a chance event, or a deadline behave as promised. Replace the guaranteed result with your next contribution.

Instead of “I will get the job,” try “I will prepare one clear example of my best work.” Instead of “Everything will work out,” try “I can name what is uncertain and choose my next step.”

This keeps the affirmation connected to reflection and action rather than prediction.

## Test it in a real moment

Read the sentence once before the situation it is meant to support. Then ask:

1. Does this still feel honest?
2. Is the action clear enough to begin?
3. Is the wording kind without avoiding reality?

Edit any phrase that is vague, pressuring, or outside your control. A useful affirmation can change as your circumstances change.

## Build a small practice around it

Choose one affirmation for the day or week. Put it somewhere you will meet it at the relevant moment, then pair it with the action it names. At the end of the day, note what you did, what got in the way, and whether the sentence needs revising.
